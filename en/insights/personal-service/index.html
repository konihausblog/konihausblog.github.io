Personal Service

September 10, 2026   ·   5 min read
Share

Good Advice Is Hard to Find

Ask around about smart home advice and you will notice a pattern. Independent, personal guidance is either hard to find or expensive. Most households end up piecing it together from product pages, forums and video tutorials.

That is odd, because the technology itself is rarely the hard part. The hard part is that nobody is responsible for the whole picture.

Advice That Ends at the Checkout

A visit to a large furniture or electronics store can be a fine start. Staff will often explain a product, and with some luck you leave with a useful tip. But once the product is paid for, you are mostly on your own with the manual. Where installation or support is offered at all, it is typically a paid extra, and it covers only that one manufacturer's products.

That is not a criticism of the people behind the counter. It is simply where their job ends.

Every Trade Adds Its Own Piece

Then there are the specialists who bring "smart" along with their own work:

  • The company that installs your shades offers a remote control, and calls it smart.
  • The company that fits your roof window offers its own motor and its own app.
  • The electrician installs everything from smart doorbells to lighting.
  • The plumber may screw smart thermostats onto your radiators, if that is part of the job at all.

Each of them does their part well. But each part comes with its own app, its own logic and its own limits, and none of them is responsible for making the pieces work together. When something does not, everyone points at the next trade.

The Real Cost Is Frustration

For someone who is not a technology enthusiast, this is exhausting. One app, then another. A device that works on Monday and not on Thursday. Nobody to ask. It is easy to lose motivation, and the attitude becomes: make it work, or forget about it.

Half-finished setups end up in a drawer, and the promise of a smarter home turns into one more chore.

It Does Not Have to Be Like That

A smart home is not supposed to be rocket science. Configuring and maintaining one properly is a craft, but for someone who does it regularly, it does not take much effort. Curious people can understand the basics quickly, and over time many do more and more on their own.

Even so, there is real convenience in having someone who guides you, and who is there when something unexpected happens. That is what personal service means: not doing everything for you forever, but staying beside you while you need it.

What Personal Service Means at Konihaus

No call centre. No ticket system. When you call or write to Konihaus, you reach me, Koni.

I install, set up and look after your smart home personally, from the first consultation to ongoing support. This is how technical support should be.

In practice, that means one person from start to finish. The same person who listens to what you actually want to make easier, chooses the devices with you, sets everything up and stays available afterwards. You never have to explain your home twice.

Personal does not mean an emergency service, and no system can promise that nothing will ever go wrong. But when something does, or when you simply have a question, you know exactly whom to ask.
