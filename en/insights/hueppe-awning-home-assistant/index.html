Controlling a Hüppe awning from Home Assistant: what worked and what didn't

November 22, 2026   ·   7 min read   ·   by Koni
Share

I wanted my two Hüppe awnings in Home Assistant without replacing the manufacturer's controller. A Somfy Izymo wired behind the awning's power unit did it in the end. Getting there took a few days and a few wrong turns, and I'm glad I found them with a jumper wire and not a soldering iron.

First idea: a module behind the panel

My two awnings are Hüppe units with a Minisonic MC operating panel on the wall. The panel handles up and down and listens to a wind sensor and a sun sensor. I wanted to open and close both awnings from Home Assistant, on a schedule or by the sun, without losing the wind protection.

The usual advice is to replace the controller with a smart one. I didn't want to. Retracting in wind is a safety function, and the manufacturer's electronics already handle it.

I had a spare Somfy Izymo Shutter io in a drawer and a TaHoma already running. The Izymo has two inputs for an existing up/down switch (A and B) and two outputs for the motor (L1 and L2). So my first plan looked simple: the Minisonic stays, its outputs feed the Izymo's inputs, and the Izymo drives the motor. Every command, wind retract included, would still pass through, and Home Assistant would get a radio path on top.

Opening the panel: it's only a controller

So I started at the wall.

On the left is the panel as it hangs in the hallway. With the cover plate off (right), there is only the control board: the display, the six push buttons and a programming header.

Behind that sits the base module, a Hüppe HSOMO (Rev 2.1). It has a small 230 V to 9 V transformer rated at only 1.5 VA, two relays, a mains terminal (L1, N), a terminal strip marked 1–8 and one marked 14–17, with "MOTOR" printed next to it.

"MOTOR" looked like exactly what I needed. It wasn't. The wires on 14–17 are thin signal wires, and the panel also takes the wind and sun sensor wires. The Minisonic is the brain, not the muscle: it sends low-voltage commands somewhere else, and that somewhere switches the 230 V motor.

That ended plan A. The Izymo's inputs need mains phase, so they can't sit on the panel's signal wires. I had to find where the 230 V motor cable actually lands.

Finding the 230 V side

The answer was outside, under the awnings: a yellow box under each one, with a Hüppe Basomat 02.100 power unit inside. Two awnings mean two identical units, and the Minisonic cable reaches both.

Inside the box, the left terminal block carries 230 V and the motor outputs. The right block is the 24 V control side, with a small transformer (22 V AC, 82 mA) and a relay with a 24 V DC coil. The Minisonic cable lands on three of the low-voltage terminals: +24 V, up and down. The colours are white, blue and green, the same as on 14–17 behind the panel.

Checking my options

With the Basomat found, I had two ways in: the 24 V control inputs, or the 230 V motor side. The 24 V side looked safer, so I tested that first.

What the 24 V inputs taught me

Before buying anything, I tested the control inputs by briefly bridging +24 V (terminal 24) to each one with a jumper wire. The box also carries 230 V, so do this only with an electrician.

My first touch lasted a fraction of a second. The relay clicked twice, once on and once off, and the awning didn't move. That told me the inputs follow the contact while it is held. The full results:

Bridge 24 V to Input What happens
25 ZB↑ (up, central) The awning goes up only while I hold the wire
26 ZB↓ (down, central) The awning goes down only while I hold the wire
22 EB↓ (down, individual) A 1 s touch starts the run, and only this awning moves
21 EB↑ (up, individual) A 1 s touch starts the run up; a 0.1 s touch stops it

So ZB behaves like a held button, and EB like a push button: a short touch stops, a longer touch starts. EB reaches only the awning it is wired to, which is why I read it as the individual input. With the awning fully retracted, "up" does nothing, because the motor's own end limit stops it.

Why the parts I had at home didn't fit

Those inputs need a dry contact: a relay whose contacts are electrically isolated and only connect two wires. I had several smart relays in a drawer, and the labels showed quickly which ones qualify.

  • Sonoff ZBMINI and ZBMINIR2: 100–240 V in and out, and the R2 says "Neutral Wire Required". They switch mains, so they can't drive a 24 V input.
  • JOIN 23-203C (Wi-Fi shutter module): 220–240 V input and a 3 A motor rating. The label doesn't say which terminals are outputs, so I left it alone.
  • Shelly 1 v3: a potential-free relay output, 16 A, and it runs from 12 V DC. This is the type that fits.

Sonoff also makes dry-contact versions, the MINI-ZBD for Zigbee and the Mini-D for Wi-Fi and Matter. The Mini-D costs about CHF 14 at Galaxus and takes 12–48 V DC or 230 V AC, with contacts rated for 24 V DC at 2 A.

The lesson: before buying a relay for a low-voltage input, look for "dry contact" or "potential-free" in the spec, and read the supply voltage on the label.

The route that worked: back to the Izymo, behind the Basomat

In the end I came back to my first idea, the Izymo, but in the right place. Instead of behind the panel, it sits on the 230 V motor side, behind the Basomat. The Basomat still receives every Minisonic command, wind retract included, and the Izymo passes it on to the motor. The Izymo also listens to Home Assistant through my TaHoma, over io-homecontrol.

The wiring, with the breaker off and an electrician at hand:

Izymo wire Goes to
A (purple) Basomat terminal 8, the up command
B (orange) Basomat terminal 9, the down command
L1 (black) Motor, black wire (up)
L2 (brown) Motor, brown wire (down)
L (brown) and N (blue) The box's own phase and neutral terminals

The two brown wires look identical, so tag them. My first attempt had the Izymo's inputs and outputs on the wrong side. After I swapped them, it worked straight away.

The Izymo's inputs A and B take mains phase, not a dry contact. Somfy's manual says they are not isolated from the mains, which is why the panel's signal wires and the 24 V side were never an option for this module.

The manual also sets limits: one motor per module, a 2 A supply with a 2 A curve C breaker upstream, and indoor use only (IP20). Those are jobs for the electrician.

Pairing and calibration

The TaHoma Switch didn't find the Izymo at first. Somfy's manual describes a pairing mode (hold PRG until the green LED stays on, for 10 minutes) and a reset for a module that was paired before (hold PRG for more than 7 seconds).

Once it was paired, the awning moved in both directions but stopped short: about 10–12 cm before the top and 30–35 cm before the bottom. The app showed "Open at 50%" whatever the real position. With a stopwatch I measured 47 seconds to open and 50 seconds to close.

The Izymo works out position from time. The manual says it needs two full up-and-down cycles to learn the end limits, and Somfy staff say on their forum that the motor has to stop by itself at both limits during those cycles. I ran full rounds through the Minisonic. After several of them, the app switched from "Open at 50%" to "Opened", and the awning now reaches the end in both directions. I can't tell which run did it.

The manual covers roller shutters, not awnings, and warns that position "may not be exact depending on the motor". For open and close that doesn't matter, because the motor's own limits stop the awning. I wouldn't count on precise positions in between.

What I would do differently

  • Open the panel before planning. A "MOTOR" label on a controller doesn't mean the motor is connected there. Follow the cable to where the 230 V actually is.
  • Test with a jumper first. A few seconds of bridging +24 V to each input told me more than any datasheet.
  • Read "dry contact" and the supply voltage on every relay before ordering it.
  • Keep the manufacturer's controller in the safety chain. With the Izymo behind the Basomat, the Minisonic's commands still pass through.
  • Expect a calibration phase. A module made for roller shutters needed several full runs to learn a 47–50 s awning.

Still on my list: testing the wind retract with the awning fully out, the 2 A protection and a dry enclosure for the Izymo, bringing the awning into Home Assistant, and the second awning, which is still on the Minisonic only.

A word on safety

Wiring on a fixed 230 V installation belongs to a licensed electrician. I'm not one, so read this as a story of what worked for me, not as an installation guide. If you want to attempt something similar, have a professional do the 230 V side, and test the wind retract before you trust it.
